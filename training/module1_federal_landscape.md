# MODULE 1 — Navigating the New Federal Landscape
**PDF schematic (participant edition outline + facilitator notes)**

## Unit 1.1 — Who Actually Controls Federal Money (30 min)
- The plumbing in plain words: Congress votes money → **OMB releases it in installments** ("apportionment") → agencies spend it → grant officers deliver it
- 2026 reality: political appointees now personally sign fund releases; **a funded program is not a flowing program**
- What this means for you: delays are structural, not personal — apply early, never let one pending application be your only plan
- **Activity:** find your community's biggest federal-funded program (school, clinic, housing) on usaspending.gov

## Unit 1.2 — The Workforce Change That Touches Everything (30 min)
- "Schedule Policy/Career" (formerly Schedule F) in one page: ~50,000 policy-influencing federal jobs lost removal protections (final rule Mar 2026, EO Jun 2026; lawsuits pending)
- Why you feel it: experienced grant/benefits staff leave → slower processing, less consistent answers
- Federal employment as a Black middle-class ladder — what shrinking it means regionally, and the pivot: state/county/municipal employment, hospital systems, utilities (similar stability, different rulebook)
- **Rule of thumb taught:** get every agency answer **in writing**; personnel churn makes verbal guidance worthless

## Unit 1.3 — Where Decisions Moved: Follow Them to the States (45 min)
- Block-grant logic: federal strings removed → **state capitals and county boards decide**
- Live examples: Title I administration (now at Dept. of Labor, phase-out proposed), TANF state plans, Medicaid waivers, HUD's *optional* per-PHA work requirements (proposed Mar 2026), ECCA opt-in governors
- The empowerment flip: a state legislator answers constituent mail; a school board seats 5–9 people elected by a few thousand votes; a PHA board meets in public
- **Activity:** locate your state plan documents (TANF, Medicaid, ESSA) + your PHA's board meeting calendar

## Unit 1.4 — Your Four Permanent Levers (45 min)
1. **Public comment** (regulations.gov) — every major rule has a comment window; agencies must respond to substantive comments; template provided
2. **Fair hearings & appeals** — benefit denials carry appeal rights (SNAP/Medicaid/TANF/housing); aid often continues if you appeal within ~10 days; scripts provided
3. **Complaints that still work in 2026:** HUD FHEO (housing discrimination, 1-yr), EEOC (employment, 180/300 days), DOJ Civil Rights (schools — moved from ED), state civil-rights agencies (often stronger), CFPB portal (still live), state attorneys general
4. **The records habit** — Nia's documentation checklist; FOIA/state open-records for program decisions
- **Capstone:** each participant files one real action (comment, records request, or watchdog signup)

## Unit 1.5 — Early-Warning System (facilitator-led setup, 30 min)
- Free alerts: Federal Register agency alerts, congress.gov bill tracking, state legislature trackers, CRS reports, court injunction trackers
- What each February's President's Budget tells you 8 months early
- Community roles: one "monitor" per congregation/block club feeding a shared alert list
