# MODULE 4 — Community-Led Institutional Navigation
**PDF schematic — the scenario playbook. Format: Situation → What changed → First 3 moves → Who to call → Deadline warnings.**
*(Each Q&A below becomes a one-page card in the PDF; cards double as Nia intake test scenarios.)*

## SECTION A — BENEFITS

**Q1. "My SNAP office says I now have work requirements — I'm 58."**
Changed: OBBBA raised the able-bodied-adult work rule to age 64 (80 documented hrs/month work, volunteering, or approved training).
Moves: (1) Check exemptions — caregiver of a child/incapacitated person, medically unfit (get the doctor's note), veteran, homeless in many states. (2) If non-exempt, enroll in a WIOA-approved training program or apprenticeship — **training hours count** and build your exit. (3) Report hours monthly with screenshots; never miss a recertification.
Call: state SNAP agency; 211; legal aid for wrongful termination. Deadline: appeal any cutoff immediately — benefits often continue pending a timely appeal.

**Q2. "My housing authority announced work requirements and time limits."**
Changed: HUD's March 2026 proposed rule lets each PHA *choose* work requirements/term limits (elderly, disabled, HUD-VASH exempt).
Moves: (1) Get the PHA's written policy — it's local and optional, and it may not have adopted anything. (2) Comment at the public PHA board meeting; policies are shapeable at this level. (3) If non-exempt: same training-counts strategy as Q1; request the hardship exemption process in writing.
Call: PHA; local legal aid housing unit; National Low Income Housing Coalition state partner. Deadline: request an **informal hearing** (federal right) before any termination takes effect.

**Q3. "My Medicaid was cut off for 'non-compliance' but I'm a caregiver."**
Changed: community-engagement (work) requirements phasing in; most losses are **paperwork failures, not eligibility failures** (Arkansas precedent).
Moves: (1) File the fair-hearing appeal within 10 days — coverage usually continues pending appeal. (2) Submit the exemption documentation (caregiver status) with proof of delivery. (3) Set a monthly reporting reminder even when exempt.
Call: state Medicaid agency; legal aid (free representation at hearings); FQHC patient navigator (findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov) for care in the gap.

**Q4. "Head Start funding in my county is delayed/ended. My kid needs pre-K."**
Moves: (1) Dual-waitlist immediately: state pre-K, Title I pre-K where offered, church/community programs. (2) From Jan 2027: ECCA-funded scholarships can cover early-learning expenses in opted-in states. (3) Get your child's developmental/IEP records in writing NOW — IDEA rights follow the child to any setting. (4) Home-based care co-op with other parents (Module 2 shows licensing as income).

## SECTION B — MONEY & CONSUMER

**Q5. "A lender is clearly ripping me off. Isn't there a federal agency for this?"**
Changed: CFPB enforcement largely paused, but its **complaint portal still operates** and forces a company response.
Moves: (1) File the CFPB complaint anyway (paper trail + response clock). (2) File with your **state attorney general** consumer unit — many now do the enforcement. (3) Check your state's usury cap and licensing status of the lender. (4) Small claims court for amounts under your state cap — no lawyer needed.
Call: state AG; legal aid consumer unit; NCLC referrals.

**Q6. "My federal grant/program (community org) lost funding or the terms changed midstream."**
Moves: (1) Read the termination notice against your award terms — improper terminations are appealable and have been reversed in court; note the appeal window. (2) Reframe and reapply under current NOFO language (opportunity/workforce/family-stability vocabulary — see policy matrix §F). (3) Diversify: community foundations, United Way, corporate giving (supplier-diversity and place-based programs), ECCA-adjacent education donors, CDFI program-related investments. (4) Join a coalition — orgs that survived 2025–26 did it in coalitions with shared back-office.
Call: your program officer (in writing); Council of Nonprofits state association; pro bono counsel via your bar's nonprofit project.

**Q7. "I want to start a business but the 'minority business' program I heard about is gone."**
Changed: race-specific program labels are being removed; the *money rails* (SBA guaranteed loans, CDFIs, SSBCI, microloans) survived.
Moves: Module 2 ladder — EIN → bank account → 6 clean months → microloan/CDFI → 7(a). NMSDC certification (private) still opens corporate supplier programs.

## SECTION C — WORK

**Q8. "I reported pay discrimination and got my first bad review two weeks later."**
Unchanged: **Title VII retaliation claims are fully alive** and among the most-won at EEOC.
Moves: (1) Timeline document tonight (Nia's documentation checklist — the *timing* is evidence). (2) EEOC charge within 180 days (300 in deferral states) — file online. (3) Dual-file with the state agency. (4) Keep performing and documenting; don't quit before advice.
Call: EEOC 1-800-669-4000; state civil-rights agency; employment lawyer on contingency.

**Q9. "My employer reclassified me as an independent contractor. Same job, no benefits."**
Moves: (1) Misclassification test — who controls hours/methods/tools? (2) Wage claim: DOL WHD 1-866-487-9243 (status-blind) + state labor agency. (3) If it stands, run the Module 2.4 self-benefits checklist so nothing lapses (especially health coverage windows).

**Q10. "I'm a federal worker whose job just moved to Schedule Policy/Career."**
Moves: (1) Nothing changes about your work today; removal protections changed. Save performance records off-site (personal copies of SF-50s, appraisals). (2) Know the litigation is live — status changes; union/MSPB avenues vary. (3) Parallel-path: state/county/municipal postings mirror many federal roles.
Call: your union local; MSPB (where jurisdiction remains); FEEA for financial hardship.

## SECTION D — EDUCATION

**Q11. "My kid's school civil-rights complaint used to go to ED. Now what?"**
Moves: File with **DOJ Civil Rights Division** (education section) AND your state civil-rights agency simultaneously — the state route is often faster and preserves your record. Keep the school's written responses; deadlines run from each incident.

**Q12. "I heard there's new scholarship money for K-12. How do I actually get it?"**
ECCA path (from Jan 2027, opted-in states): governor opt-in check → find qualifying SGOs → apply early (first-come) → use for tuition/tutoring/tech/transport/special-needs → keep every receipt. Income limit: ≤300% of area median — most working families qualify.

**Q13. "My student loan servicer changed (Treasury transfer) and my payment count looks wrong."**
Moves: (1) Compare against the StudentAid.gov file you downloaded (Module 3 made you do this). (2) Written dispute to servicer + CFPB complaint + state AG. (3) Never go delinquent over a dispute — request administrative forbearance in writing while it's resolved.

**Q14. "TRIO/Upward Bound at my college 'paused.' I'm first-gen and counting on it."**
The authorizing law is unchanged; administration moved to Labor. Moves: written request to the campus program director for continuation plan; dean of students; college-access nonprofits (UNCF, local access networks) as bridge; document promised services.

## SECTION E — IMMIGRATION-ADJACENT (max-privacy handling; never store these cases)

**Q15. "Our housing authority is asking about my family's immigration status."**
A mixed-status rule is *proposed*, contested, and status-dependent. Move: **do not respond before speaking to legal aid** — deadlines on PHA notices matter, but so does not self-reporting. Call: local immigration legal aid (immigrationlawhelp.org) + fair-housing org same day. (Nia handles these conversations in maximum-privacy mode: nothing stored.)

## SECTION F — THE META-CARD (back cover)

**When ANY program changes, run the same five questions:**
1. Is this a *proposed* rule, a *final* rule, or a *law*? (Proposed = comment window; final = litigation/compliance; law = state-plan details still vary)
2. Is there an exemption, and what documents prove I fit it?
3. What's my appeal deadline, and does aid continue if I appeal on time?
4. Which *state or local* body now holds the decision — and when does it meet?
5. What's the non-governmental parallel path (CDFI, SGO, union, church network, mutual aid)?

*Every card cross-references the Nia resource directory. Update quarterly; this landscape moves monthly.*
