# Your Rights During Pregnancy Care — Plain Language Guide
**Last Updated:** 2026-07-14
**Version:** 0.1
**Reading Level Target:** 6th grade
**Review Status:** NOT YET REVIEWED — needs review by a legal aid or medical-legal partner before deployment

> Black women in America die from pregnancy-related causes at about
> **3 times the rate** of white women. Most of these deaths are
> **preventable**. Being dismissed by medical staff is not something you
> have to accept. You have rights, and this guide is how you use them.

## What Are Your Rights?
When you are pregnant and you go to a hospital or clinic:

- **You have the right to be examined in an emergency.** Any emergency
  room that takes Medicare (almost all of them) MUST check you and
  treat you until you are stable. This includes labor. They must do this
  even if you have no insurance, no money, or no papers. This law is
  called EMTALA.
- **You have the right to be treated the same as any other patient.**
  A hospital or doctor that gets federal money (almost all of them)
  cannot treat you worse because of your race. That is illegal.
- **You have the right to ask questions and get answers** about your
  body, your baby, and any test or treatment — before you say yes to it.
- **You have the right to say no** to a treatment, and the right to ask
  for a different doctor or nurse.
- **You have the right to a second opinion** and the right to leave and
  go to a different hospital (unless it is not safe to travel).

## Warning Signs — Take These Seriously
If you are pregnant (or gave birth within the last year) and have any of
these, get care **right away** and use the exact words below:

- Headache that is very bad or will not go away
- Swelling in your face or hands
- Changes in your vision (blurry, spots, flashes)
- Chest pain or trouble breathing
- Heavy bleeding
- Fever
- Your baby moving much less than before
- Feeling that "something is wrong" — this counts

## What To Say When You Are Being Dismissed
These exact sentences make staff act, because they create a record:

1. **"I am pregnant and I have [symptom]. I want this checked before I leave."**
2. **"I am asking you to document in my chart that I reported [symptom]
   and that you are declining to test/treat it."** (Staff often act
   instead of writing that down.)
3. **"I want to speak to the charge nurse / patient advocate."**
   Every hospital has one. Ask by name.
4. If it is an emergency: **"I am asking for the medical screening exam
   I have a right to under EMTALA."**

Bring someone with you when you can. A witness changes how you are treated.

## What Counts as Evidence?
If you think you were dismissed or treated worse because of your race:
- Write down dates, times, and the names of every person you talked to
- Write the exact words they used, as soon as you can
- Ask for a copy of your medical records — you have a legal right to them
- Keep names of anyone who was with you

## What Can You Do?
1. **In the moment:** ask for the patient advocate or charge nurse.
   Say the sentences above.
2. **File a complaint with the hospital** — ask for the patient
   relations office. Put it in writing.
3. **File a discrimination complaint with the federal government** —
   HHS Office for Civil Rights: **1-800-368-1019** or hhs.gov/ocr.
   Free. You do not need a lawyer to file.
4. **Report an emergency-room refusal** (EMTALA violation) to your
   state health department.
5. **Talk to a lawyer** — medical civil-rights lawyers often work free
   ("contingency") and only get paid if you win.

## Time Limits — IMPORTANT
- HHS Office for Civil Rights complaint: **180 days** from the incident
- State medical board complaints: varies, sooner is better
- Don't wait — records get harder to get and memories fade
