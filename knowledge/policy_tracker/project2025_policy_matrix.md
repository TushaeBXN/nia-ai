# PHASE 2 — Policy Alteration & Impact Matrix
## What Changes, Where It Bites, Where the Openings Are, and How to Navigate
**Source:** Mandate for Leadership chapters 10–27 + verified implementation status July 2026
**Last Updated:** 2026-07-14 | **Reading frame:** every entry = Change → Vulnerability → Opportunity → Navigation

---

## A. EDUCATION (Ch. 11, Lindsey Burke)

### A1. Dismantling the Department of Education
- **Proposed change:** Distribute ED programs to other agencies; Title I, Part A converted to "no-strings" block grant, then **state revenue responsibility phased in over 10 years** (i.e., federal Title I money ends); statistics office to Census; Indian education to BIE.
- **Verified status (July 2026):** ED still exists (abolition needs Congress) but is at ~2,800 staff (from 4,100+), with **14 interagency agreements**: Title I + TRIO → **Dept. of Labor**; student loan portfolio → **Treasury (in phases)**; special education → **HHS**; school civil-rights enforcement → **DOJ**.
- **Vulnerability:** Title I serves ~26M students in low-income districts. A block grant without targeting rules lets states redirect money away from the poorest schools; a 10-year phase-out ends it. TRIO (college access for first-gen students) now sits in an agency with no history running it — service gaps during transfer.
- **Opportunity:** Block grants also mean **state capitals decide** — a school board and a state legislator are far more reachable than Congress. Organized parent groups have real leverage at that level.
- **Navigation:** (1) Learn your state's Title I distribution formula now — state education agency publishes it. (2) File complaints that used to go to ED's OCR with **DOJ Civil Rights Division** (and simultaneously with your state civil-rights agency — dual filing preserves deadlines). (3) TRIO participants: get everything in writing from program staff; the *authorizing statute is unchanged* — services are still owed.

### A2. Student loans
- **Proposed change:** Restore lending "to the private sector" or make the federal government guarantor only; end "abuses" of loan forgiveness (PSLF targeted); consolidate income-driven repayment into one less-generous plan.
- **Status:** Portfolio moving to Treasury in phases; OBBBA (July 2025) consolidated new-borrower repayment into two plans (standard + "Repayment Assistance Plan"); PSLF narrowed by regulation for certain employers — litigation ongoing.
- **Vulnerability:** Black borrowers hold disproportionate student debt (typical Black bachelor's graduate owes more 4 years *after* graduation than at graduation). Servicer chaos during the Treasury transfer = missed-payment damage to credit scores through no fault of the borrower.
- **Opportunity:** The Mandate itself pushes **alternatives to the BA**: apprenticeships and CTE "on an even playing field with degrees" — those funding streams are growing while loan generosity shrinks.
- **Navigation:** (1) Download your full loan history from **StudentAid.gov NOW** (payment counts, employer certifications) — records get lost in transfers; screenshots are appeal evidence. (2) Recertify IDR/PSLF employment every year, not at forgiveness time. (3) Dispute servicer errors in writing + CFPB complaint portal (still operational) + state attorney general. (4) Before borrowing: price an apprenticeship or employer-paid pathway first (Module 3).

### A3. School choice expansion
- **Proposed change:** Universal D.C. Opportunity Scholarship; federal encouragement of state ESAs; portability of federal funds.
- **Status:** **ECCA/Federal Scholarship Tax Credit is LAW** (enacted July 4, 2025; live Jan 1, 2027): $1,700 federal credit for donations to Scholarship Granting Organizations; families ≤300% of area median income eligible; **30 states opted in** as of mid-2026; governor must opt in annually.
- **Vulnerability:** Public school funding erodes where enrollment drops; rural families and students with disabilities may find few private options; SGO scholarship supply depends on donor behavior.
- **Opportunity:** This is a **new capital stream low-income families can actually claim**: scholarships for tuition, tutoring, books, transportation, educational technology, special-needs services — not just private school tuition.
- **Navigation:** (1) Check whether your governor opted in (list updates annually). (2) Locate qualifying SGOs in-state and apply EARLY — scholarships are first-come. (3) Homeschool co-ops and microschools qualify for many expense categories — a community org can run one. (4) Keep receipts for every qualified expense category.

## B. LABOR (Ch. 18, Jonathan Berry)

### B1. Removal of DEI apparatus + disparate-impact liability
- **Proposed change:** EO banning racial classifications/CRT training; **"Eliminate disparate impact liability"** (would require Congress for Title VII; agencies can stop *enforcing* it); EEOC to stop collecting EEO-1 race/ethnicity pay data.
- **Status:** EOs 14151/14173 (Jan 2025) ended federal DEI programs and revoked EO 11246 (federal-contractor affirmative action, in force since 1965); OFCCP largely stood down; disparate-impact enforcement deprioritized by EO (April 2025). **Title VII itself is unchanged — intentional discrimination remains fully illegal.**
- **Vulnerability:** Pattern-based (statistical) discrimination claims get harder; contractor workforce diversity reporting disappears, removing the early-warning data.
- **Opportunity:** None to spin — but the *individual* claim path is intact and now the main path.
- **Navigation:** (1) Teach workers the difference: **disparate treatment (intentional) is still illegal and enforceable** — EEOC charge within 180/300 days, private right of action after right-to-sue. (2) Documentation becomes decisive: dates, words, comparators (Nia's documentation checklist). (3) **State civil-rights agencies** in ~25 states enforce their own disparate-impact standards — file state charges where available. (4) Private class-action bar still litigates pattern cases — legal aid can refer.

### B2. Independent contractor / gig classification
- **Proposed change:** Restore Trump-era test making contractor status easier; protect "60 million" independent workers.
- **Vulnerability:** Contractors get no overtime, workers' comp, unemployment insurance, or employer FICA — misclassification quietly strips a family's safety net.
- **Opportunity:** Legitimate independent work is a real income ladder (Kelly's domain: side businesses, trades).
- **Navigation:** Know the tells of misclassification (employer sets hours/methods, provides tools, sole client). Wage claims for misclassified workers via DOL WHD (1-866-487-9243, status-blind) and state labor agencies. If genuinely independent: quarterly taxes, solo 401(k)/SEP-IRA, and liability structure (LLC) become *your* benefits department — teach them.

### B3. Apprenticeships & workforce programs
- **Proposed change:** Revive **Industry-Recognized Apprenticeship Programs (IRAPs)** alongside the 80-year-old Registered Apprenticeship Program; reassess the ~$17B/43-program federal training portfolio as "outcome-based"; expand licensing compacts.
- **Status:** Apprenticeship funding expanded (~$285M registered apprenticeship + new youth grants proposed in WIOA reauthorization "ASWA 2026," pending Senate).
- **Vulnerability:** IRAPs historically lacked the wage-progression and quality guarantees of Registered Apprenticeships; Job Corps (residential program serving mostly low-income youth of color) faced a 2025 shutdown attempt (paused by courts) — instability is the risk.
- **Opportunity:** **This is the single biggest sanctioned pathway in the whole document.** Earn-while-you-learn, no debt, credential + wage progression: median completed-apprentice starting salary ≈ $80k in some trades.
- **Navigation:** apprenticeship.gov finder; union apprenticeship programs (IBEW, UA, carpenters) pay during training and are covered in Module 3; community colleges' pre-apprenticeship courses; verify any IRAP's outcomes data before enrolling — RAP listing is the quality floor.

## C. HOUSING — HUD (Ch. 15, Ben Carson)

| Change | Status (Jul 2026) | Vulnerability | Navigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Repeal AFFH** (Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing rule) | Repealed again 2025 | Localities lose the mandate to analyze segregation patterns; **individual FHA complaints unaffected** | Fair Housing Act complaints still run: HUD 1-800-669-9777, 1-year deadline; local fair-housing orgs still do testing (nationalfairhousing.org) |
| **Work requirements + time limits** on public housing/vouchers | **Proposed rule March 2, 2026** — optional per-PHA; elderly, disabled, HUD-VASH exempt | Non-exempt tenants risk losing assistance mid-lease; term limits push families out before wages catch up | Know your PHA: rules are LOCAL and optional — attend PHA board meetings (public), demand written policy, use hardship-exemption processes, appeal every adverse decision (informal hearing right is federal) |
| **Bar mixed-status families** from assisted housing | Re-proposed | A citizen child can lose housing because a parent lacks status | Immigration + housing = max-privacy domain in Nia. Route to legal aid BEFORE responding to any PHA status inquiry; NILC and local orgs track the rule's status |
| **Shift from public housing to vouchers; FHA shorter-duration mortgages as "wealth-building"** | Ongoing | Voucher acceptance by landlords is the choke point | Source-of-income discrimination is illegal in ~20 states/many cities — check local law; FHA 15/20-year products, where offered, genuinely build equity faster **if** payment fits ≤31% of income — run the math before signing |

## D. WELFARE & HEALTH — HHS (Ch. 14) + SNAP (Ch. 10)

- **TANF:** re-weight toward work + marriage-promotion priorities; explicit measurement of family-formation goals. **Navigation:** TANF is a block grant — your *state plan* is the real rulebook (public document); fair-hearing rights on any denial are federal and unchanged.
- **Medicaid:** Section 1115 **work requirements** for able-bodied adults. **Status:** OBBBA imposed national community-engagement requirements (phasing in ~2026–27). **Navigation:** exemptions (caregivers, students, medically frail) exist but are *documentation-heavy* — the coverage losses in the Arkansas experiment were mostly paperwork failures, not eligibility failures. Teach: report hours monthly even when exempt, keep screenshots, appeal every termination (aid continues pending appeal if filed within 10 days in most states).
- **SNAP:** **LIVE:** ABAWD work-requirement age raised to 64 (OBBBA); 80 hrs/month work/volunteer/training, documented. **Navigation:** approved *training* counts — enrolling in a WIOA program or apprenticeship satisfies the requirement AND builds the exit ramp; volunteering counts; homeless individuals and veterans have exemptions in many states.
- **Head Start:** Mandate proposes **elimination** ($11B program). Congress has not eliminated it; funding delays occurred 2025–26. **Navigation:** dual-enroll waitlists (Head Start + state pre-K + ECCA-funded options from 2027); document developmental services in writing so IDEA rights carry into any new setting.
- **Childcare:** priority to **home-based care**, on-site employer care incentives. **Opportunity:** home daycare is a licensed *business* a low-income woman can own — CDFI microloans + state licensing courses; Module 2 covers it.

## E. MONEY & CAPITAL — Treasury (Ch. 22), SBA (Ch. 25), SEC/CFPB (Ch. 27), Fed (Ch. 24)

### E1. Treasury / IRS
- **Change:** Two-bracket income tax (15%/30%) intention; corporate rate to 18%; **supermajority requirement to raise taxes**; eliminate Treasury equity offices (Counselor for Racial Equity, Advisory Committee on Racial Equity, DEIA office).
- **Vulnerability:** The 30% bracket would start near the median wage — many working families' marginal rates *rise*; EITC/CTC claiming support (VITA outreach) weakens as equity offices close.
- **Navigation:** **Free tax filing + full credit claiming is a four-figure annual event for a low-income family**: VITA sites (irs.gov/vita), EITC ≈ up to ~$7,800, CTC, Saver's Credit. About 1 in 5 EITC-eligible workers never claims — teach the claim, every year, with prior-3-year amended returns where missed.

### E2. SBA
- **Change:** End **direct lending**; keep guaranteed lending through partners (7(a), 504, Microloan — 62,000 loans/yr); raise 7(a) ceiling to ~$50M for manufacturing; strengthen Office of Advocacy; anti-fraud cleanup after $78B potentially fraudulent EIDL.
- **Vulnerability:** Direct lending's end + fraud-scarred programs = tighter underwriting; businesses without banking relationships (disproportionately Black-owned: ~half as likely to get full requested financing) get squeezed at the margin.
- **Opportunity:** Guaranteed programs SURVIVE — and the alternative rails are growing:
  - **CDFIs** (1,400+ community lenders, mission-bound to underserved borrowers) — find via ofn.org locator
  - **SBA Microloans** (≤$50k, through nonprofit intermediaries, character-based underwriting)
  - **SSBCI** state small-business credit programs ($10B, state-administered)
  - **Revenue-based financing + credit unions**
- **Navigation:** Module 2 sequence: EIN → business bank account → 6 months clean statements → microloan/CDFI → graduate to 7(a). SBDCs and SCORE mentoring remain free.

### E3. SEC / capital formation (openings written into the text)
- **Change:** Streamline **Regulation A** (small public offerings) and **Reg CF (crowdfunding)**; preempt state blue-sky registration; **broaden or eliminate the accredited-investor restriction**; exclude crowdfunding shareholders from the S-corp 100-shareholder cap.
- **Opportunity:** These *lower the legal cost of raising community capital* — investment clubs, community-owned businesses, and small issuers benefit. A neighborhood co-op raising $1M from its own customers via Reg CF is exactly what this deregulation permits.
- **Navigation:** Reg CF via FINRA-registered portals; investment-club structure (already in Nia's wealth curriculum); state securities regulators still protect against fraud — check any offering at investor.gov.

### E4. CFPB
- **Change:** Chapter calls for abolition ("must be abolished" via Congress) / defunding.
- **Status:** Gutted 2025 (stop-work orders, terminated enforcement, staff cuts; "life support") — **but the complaint portal is operational** (5.8M complaints processed 2025; 92% forwarded to companies) with a June 2026 overhaul tightening credit-reporting complaint intake.
- **Vulnerability:** Enforcement against predatory lenders is largely paused — the market gets more dangerous exactly as families lose benefit cushions.
- **Navigation:** Complaint portal still triggers a company response clock — use it, plus: state attorneys general (many have mini-CFPB units), state usury caps, FTC complaint (reportfraud.ftc.gov), and **prevention curriculum** (spotting predatory terms) because the referee is off the field.

## F. CROSS-CUTTING TERMINOLOGY SHIFT (grant applicants must know)

The Mandate directs deletion of terms across all agencies: *DEI, diversity, equity, gender equality, abortion, reproductive rights, climate justice* etc. — and Treasury/SEC/Labor chapters each order equity-office elimination.
**Practical grant guidance:** reframe the same work in the document's own favored language — "expanding opportunity," "workforce readiness," "family stability," "community revitalization," "rural and underserved access." The work doesn't change; the vocabulary that gets funded does. Track each agency's current NOFO language before writing any application.
