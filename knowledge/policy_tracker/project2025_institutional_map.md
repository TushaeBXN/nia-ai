## 1. THE COMMAND LAYER — White House Office (Ch. 1, Rick Dearborn)

| Entity | Administrative Function | Levers of Influence | Socioeconomic Impact Vector |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chief of Staff** | Sequences all policy rollout; all senior staff report through COS | Controls which policies reach the President; coordinates OLA + Communications + policy councils | Determines *speed* of every change below — communities get little notice windows |
| **Office of Legislative Affairs (OLA)** | Liaison to Congress; moves nominations and the legislative agenda | Confirmation pipeline for Cabinet/agency heads; brokers reconciliation bills | Budget reconciliation is how benefit rules change fast (e.g., OBBBA 2025 SNAP changes) — watch OLA priorities to predict benefit-law timing |
| **Office of Political Affairs (OPA)** | Manages President's political interests; liaison to party committees | Aligns agency messaging with political calendar | Program announcements/cancellations cluster around political timing — plan applications accordingly |
| **Office of Public Liaison (OPL)** | Gateway for outside interest groups | Decides which community organizations get access | Advocacy access shifts from civil-rights orgs to advisory-board network orgs |
| **White House Counsel** | Legal clearance for executive actions | Vets EOs for litigation survival | Speed and durability of EOs (e.g., DEI rescissions) depends here; litigation windows are the community's counter-clock |
| **Cabinet Affairs** | Manages the 15 departments + agency heads | Enforces White House priorities on secretaries | Uniformity: the same policy language appears across every agency at once |

**Key mechanic to teach:** Policy no longer travels agency-by-agency. It is centrally sequenced. When one agency removes a program term (e.g., "equity" grant criteria), expect the same removal at every agency within months. **Monitoring one Federal Register docket predicts the rest.**

## 2. THE CONTROL LAYER — Executive Office of the President (Ch. 2, Russ Vought)

### OMB — the single most consequential office for low-income programs

| Mechanism | What It Does | Why It Matters to a Low-Income Household |
|---|---|---|
| **Apportionment power** (Anti-Deficiency Act, 1870) | OMB releases appropriated money to agencies in installments; the Mandate directs that political appointees (**PADs**) personally sign apportionments, not career **DADs** | Congress can fund a program and OMB can still slow/condition the money's release. **A funded program is not a flowing program.** Grant delays at community orgs trace back here |
| **Resource Management Offices (RMOs)** | Six functional units supervising all agency spending "including at many agencies wrongly regarded as independent" | Housing, health, education grant terms are edited at this desk before publication |
| **OIRA + EO 12866** | Reviews every significant regulation; Mandate extends review to **independent agencies** (per 2019 OLC opinion) | Rules that expand benefits move slowly; rules that trim them are prioritized — comment periods are the community's formal voice (anyone can file) |
| **Budget Review Division (BRD)** | Top career unit; drafts the President's Budget | The President's Budget is the earliest public warning document each February — free, public, and specific about which programs are proposed for zeroing |
| **Circular A-4 / regulatory analysis** | Cost-benefit methodology | How "burden" vs "benefit" is defined determines whether protections survive review |

### The Policy Councils

| Council | Function | Impact Vector |
|---|---|---|
| **National Economic Council (NEC)** | Principal economic policy coordination; chairs standing EOP coordination meeting | Tax, wage, small-business frameworks originate here |
| **Domestic Policy Council (DPC)** | Domestic priorities incl. a proposed "life and family" special assistant | Welfare (TANF/Medicaid/childcare) redesign coordination |
| **National Security Council (NSC)** | Security policy; Mandate directs staff cuts + alignment | Immigration enforcement posture affecting mixed-status households |
| **Office of the Vice President** | Seat at budget + regulatory review | Tie-breaking votes on reconciliation benefit bills |

## 3. THE PERSONNEL LAYER — Central Personnel Agencies (Ch. 3, Devine/Kirk/Dans)

| Entity / Authority | Function | Mandate's Change | Verified Status (July 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OPM** (Title 5 U.S.C.) | Executes/administers/enforces all civil-service rules: hiring, pay, classification, retirement | Director made direct presidential instrument, "available in his Cabinet"; merit-pay replacing seniority; benefit reductions toward private-sector parity | Reorganization ongoing; RIF authorities used broadly 2025 |
| **Schedule F → "Schedule Policy/Career"** | Strips civil-service removal protections from "policy-influencing" career positions (EO 13957 of 2020, revived) | Reinstate and expand | **LIVE: final rule effective March 9, 2026; implementing EO June 3, 2026; ~50,000 positions; litigation pending** |
| **Schedule C + noncareer SES** | Political appointee slots (~4,000 total historically) | Fill 100% on day one via PPO pipeline; noncareer SES to dominate evaluation boards | Staffed via Project 2025 personnel database |
| **Presidential Personnel Office (PPO)** | White House control of all appointments | Pre-vetted personnel database ("conservative LinkedIn") from partner orgs | Operating |
| **MSPB / FLRA / OSC** | Employee appeals; union relations; whistleblower protection | Narrow scope; curb union role | Appeals capacity reduced; union EOs litigated |

**Impact vector to teach plainly:**
1. **Federal employment itself is a mobility ladder** — the federal workforce has long over-indexed Black professionals (roughly 18% of federal workers vs ~13% of the civilian workforce). Reclassification + RIFs + return-to-office directives shrink that ladder, concentrated in DC/MD/VA, Atlanta, and other hub metros.
2. **Program administration slows before rules change** — when experienced grant officers leave, applications stall even where the law is unchanged. Teach applicants to file early, document everything, and expect longer cycles.
3. **Discretion moves to political appointees** — waivers, hardship exceptions, and enforcement priorities become less predictable; written records and formal complaint channels matter more than relationships.

## 5. STANDING MONITORING CHECKLIST (feeds Mike's Phase-2 monitors)

- **federalregister.gov** — free email alerts by agency + docket; comment portal: regulations.gov
- **President's Budget (Feb)** + agency Congressional Justifications — earliest program-cut warnings
- **OMB apportionment database** (public since 2022 statute; monitor for postings/removals)
- **congress.gov** — reconciliation bills carry benefit changes; CRS reports are free and neutral
- **State opt-in/opt-out lists** — increasingly the deciding layer (ECCA scholarship opt-ins, Medicaid waivers, SNAP waivers)
- **Court dockets** — courtlistener.com/RECAP free; injunctions repeatedly pause implementation windows
